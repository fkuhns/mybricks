From Claude Code:
I recommended both, for different jobs:

> 0.1 µF ceramic, one across each motor's two terminals, soldered right at the
  motor. This suppresses the high-frequency electrical noise from the brushes
  sparking, which is the likely cause of I2C errors that appear only while the
  motors run. It's the cheapest fix and the one I'd do first.

> 470–1000 µF electrolytic, one across the motor supply at the driver board,
  if the board has a place for it. This one holds the supply voltage up during
  current surges, like starting or reversing. It helps against supply dips,
  but does little for the high-frequency noise.

> So the motor caps target the noise and the big cap targets the dips. Your
  logs showed the 5 V rail held up fairly well (lowest 4.46 V), so I'd start
  with the 0.1 µF caps and add the bulk cap if errors remain.

> When fitting the electrolytic, match its polarity: the stripe marks the
  negative lead. Also make sure its voltage rating is above the supply
  voltage; 10 V or more is plenty for this pack.
